- Ship a GLP-1 companion mode as a first-class feature: protein-first targets, lean-mass preservation warnings, medication/dose reminders, and small-appetite meal quality guidance — up to 40% of GLP-1 weight loss is lean mass and users under-eat protein by ~40%; this is the clearest underserved, fast-growing segment.

- Design the entire onboarding and first 14 days around habit survival: ~80% of users quit within 2 weeks and only 23% log at 3 months — get a successful photo log within the first minute, default to moderate (not extreme) deficits, and make missed days forgiving rather than streak-punishing.

## flows-onboarding
- Rebuild onboarding as a quiz funnel in this exact order: goal → body stats (age/sex/height/weight) → activity level → goal weight + pace (slider with recommended band) → 'building your plan' animation → personalized plan reveal → signup last. This is the converged pattern across Cal AI, Noom, Yazio, and Flo.
- Move account creation to the very end (or make it optional until after the first log). Cal AI's single highest-leverage change was moving sign-in to the end of onboarding; store quiz answers locally/anonymous-session in Supabase and attach them at signup.
- Every quiz answer must get a response: an affirmation ('Losing 5 kg is a realistic target'), a micro-education screen, or an updated projection. Noom's rule — length isn't the problem, emptiness is. Add 2-3 'investment' questions even if they don't feed the calculation; Cal AI proved they lift conversion.
- Compute the plan with Mifflin-St Jeor × activity factor, pace → daily kcal via 7700 kcal/kg, cap deficits at ~35% of TDEE, block unrealistic goal weights, and present the result as a payoff: named plan, macro breakdown, and a date-stamped weight-projection curve ('70 kg by Oct 12'), never as raw form output.
- Keep a short (5-10s) plan-computing animation that names real steps ('calculating your metabolic rate… setting protein target…') — labor illusion is proven at Noom/Flo — but A/B its duration; one Adapty test showed removing bloated loaders lifted trials 22%.
- Deliver first value inside the first session: get the user to their first AI photo log within ~2 minutes of finishing onboarding (or even during it, with a sample photo). 86% of trial conversions happen on Day 0, and 'first successful photo log' should be the tracked first-value activation event; 'logged on 3+ distinct days in week 1' the core-value event.
- Ask for permissions in-context with branded pre-permission screens during onboarding, not at launch: notifications framed as 'meal reminders help you hit your goal' (+6.4% trial conversion, +24% ARPU in Adapty tests; ~65% opt-in with priming vs 25-35% without).
- Place the paywall (if/when added) immediately after the plan reveal at the emotional peak, with outcome-framed copy ('Reach 70 kg by October') rather than feature lists; benchmarks favor onboarding-placed paywalls, and hard paywall vs freemium is a deliberate fork (12.1% vs 2.2% conversion, but freemium wins ~15-25% more 12-month revenue via late converters).
- Add a set-expectations/trust screen mid-quiz (Cal AI's 'weight loss is delayed at first' + privacy reassurance; Noom's '0.5-1 kg/week' repetition). Honest pacing framing reduces week-2 churn from unrealistic expectations and is cheap to build.

- Make onboarding config-driven (questions/order/copy served from Supabase, not hardcoded in React) so flows can be A/B tested without redeploys — Flo's backend-driven Survey Engine yielded +13.5% ARPU and +20% onboarding conversion purely from iteration speed.
- Track onboarding-completion and activation separately — 90%+ completion can coexist with day-2 churn; instrument the funnel per screen (drop-off), plus first-photo-log rate and week-1 logging-days as the metrics that actually predict retention.
## journey-retention
- Make sub-30-second logging the core design KPI: logging time is the strongest quantified churn predictor (users >2 min/meal quit at ~3x the rate; ~80% of trackers quit within 2 weeks). AI photo logging is the means to this, but every estimate must be instantly editable with visible confidence, since photo AI runs 25-35% error on mixed dishes.
- Design explicitly for the weeks 3-10 churn cliff: logging drops from 5.4 to 1.4 days/week after week 4 in studies, and fewer than half of users still track by week 9-10. Ship targeted interventions in that window: habit milestones at day 7/30, plateau education around week 4-6, and a re-engagement flow that welcomes lapsed users back without guilt.
- Adopt adherence-neutral design (MacroFactor model): never show red warnings for going over calories, treat over- and under-target symmetrically as data, and let users pause tracking (sickness, vacation) with zero penalty. Shame is a documented churn driver - a quarter of negative social posts about top fitness apps describe shame/demotivation.
- If you use streaks, build forgiveness in from day one: streak freezes or one free miss per week, decay instead of hard reset, and celebrate 'days logged this week' and total active days over consecutive perfection. Strict streaks cause immediate abandonment after the first break (users don't rebuild, they leave). Consider counting a day as 'logged' if >=2 eating occasions are recorded - the validated adherence marker.
- Build a weekly reflection loop as the retention engine: an auto-generated weekly check-in that recalculates the user's TDEE/targets from their own weight trend + logged intake (so returning weekly has functional value), plus a Wrapped-style shareable recap with one comparative insight and one actionable suggestion. 'Weekly progress loop with comparative trends' is cited as the strongest long-term retention driver for health apps.
- Handle plateaus algorithmically and empathetically: smooth weight data into a trend line (ignore sodium/water spikes), detect stalls around weeks 4-8, then proactively explain the plateau with the user's own data and offer concrete options (maintenance phase, diet break, small target adjustment) instead of letting the user conclude they failed.
- Ship a 'bad day' recovery flow grounded in self-compassion research: after a big overage or missed days, show compassionate framing ('one day never defines a week'), no make-up penalties, and treat each week as a self-contained unit. Self-compassion prompts measurably cut post-lapse overeating by more than half and increase intention to continue.
- Cap notifications at ~2/day (1 contextual meal reminder + 1 insight), anchor reminders to the user's actual eating times, suppress them once a meal is logged, and back off after repeated ignores (JITAI principle: badly-timed prompts actively harm engagement). Never send guilt-toned nags about unlogged or 'bad' foods - notification shame is a top documented churn theme.
- Make calorie targets feel attainable and user-controlled: let users pick their rate of loss, explain how the number was derived from their own data, support maintenance mode, and never issue aggressive generic deficits (users publicly rage-quit over impossible targets like '-700 cal/day'). Avoid good/bad food color-coding - Noom's system demonstrably induces guilt and binary thinking.
- Set honest retention benchmarks and instrument them: D1 30-35%, D7 15-20%, D30 8-12% are par for health apps (top-tier: 45/30/25%). Define activation as 'logged >=2 meals/day for 7 days' and track cohort retention against the weeks 3-10 cliff specifically.
- Add lightweight accountability once the solo habit sticks (around week 3-6): friend streaks lifted Duolingo daily completion 22%, and accountability partners correlate with 2-3x D90 retention. Even a simple 'share weekly recap with a buddy' beats building a full social network.
- Differentiate on trust: Cal AI (the category leader in AI photo logging) was removed from the App Store in April 2026 for deceptive billing and leaked 3.2M user records in March 2026. Transparent pricing, clear auto-renewal terms, and a visible privacy stance (user-owned data, easy export/delete via Supabase) are now competitive features in this category.
## features

- Treat portion size, not food recognition, as the AI accuracy problem: real-world food ID is 68-86% but portion reliability drops to ~39% and Cal AI misses portions by 20-40%. - Ship retention scaffolding with known multipliers: streaks with milestone celebrations (MFP celebrates days 4-5 specifically because week-2 churn is the cliff), goal-setting at onboarding (+30% continuation), reminders (+70% engagement), and an auto-generated 7-day weekly report with food-group insights.

- Positioning gap: MFP owns the mass market (and now Cal AI), MacroFactor owns adaptive coaching, Cronometer owns micros — the open lane for CalTrack AI is 'AI-fast logging with honest accuracy + adaptive targets + GLP-1-aware protein focus' at a price under MFP's $79.99/yr.

## tech
- Use Gemini 3 Flash (Lovable gateway default) with responseSchema-constrained JSON for photo analysis: ~$0.0013 per photo when images are resized client-side, structured output works with Zod out of the box; if going direct-API, benchmark against GPT-5 mini ($0.25/$2 per M) since peer-reviewed data (Oct 2025) showed GPT-4o/Claude beating Gemini 1.5 Pro on food MAPE (36% vs 64-110%) before Gemini 3 closed the gap.
- All vision LLMs systematically underestimate calories and the error grows with portion size (bias slopes -0.23 to -0.50, MAPE ~36% even for the best models) — design for correction: per-item breakdown, confidence scores, one-tap portion adjustment, and prompt the model to estimate grams first then derive kcal.
- Compress photos client-side to <=768px longest edge (WebP/JPEG q~0.8 via browser-image-compression or createImageBitmap+canvas.toBlob) — that hits Gemini's single-tile 258-token bucket, cuts upload time ~90%, and keeps the whole AI pipeline under a fifth of a cent per log.
- Edge function pattern: Zod safeParse the request, call the model, then Zod-validate the model's JSON before any DB write (treat LLM output as untrusted); Supabase's 2s CPU limit is fine since LLM wait time is I/O, and streaming via text/event-stream ReadableStream is available but unnecessary for single-shot photo analysis.
- Supabase does not rate-limit inbound edge function calls — add per-user (auth UID, not IP) rate limiting yourself; official example uses Upstash Redis, a Postgres counter table works at small scale. Cap something like 10 AI analyses/min/user to bound spend.
- Make logging offline-tolerant with TanStack Query v5: networkMode 'offlineFirst', PersistQueryClientProvider (gcTime >= 24h maxAge), setMutationDefaults + resumePausedMutations so a meal logged offline survives a page reload and syncs later, with optimistic diary updates.
- Ship as a PWA via vite-plugin-pwa, but know iOS Safari has NO beforeinstallprompt — show a manual 'Share -> Add to Home Screen' instruction banner only when display-mode is 'browser'; use the real beforeinstallprompt event for a custom install button on Android/desktop Chrome.

## prompts
- Portion size, not food identification, is the dominant error (ID ~90% accurate; weight/energy ~36% MAPE even for the best models) — invest prompt effort in scale: enumerate reference objects (26 cm plate, 19 cm fork, 8.5 cm credit card, 150-180 ml katori) and require the model to state its `portion_basis` per item.
- All vision models systematically UNDERestimate, and more so as portions grow (bias slopes -0.23 to -0.50) — bake an explicit bias-correction step into the prompt ('if the plate is full, revise upward 15-25%') rather than trusting first estimates.
- Force step-by-step reasoning BEFORE the JSON (scene check → itemize → scale → quantify → confidence → clarify): CoT lifted NutriBench accuracy from 43% to 67% (GPT-4o) and helps most on multi-item plates, which are the weak spot (94% single-item vs 82% multi-item accuracy).
- Use the hybrid architecture production apps converged on: LLM outputs items + quantities as strict JSON, then a deterministic DB lookup (USDA FDC for Western, IFCT 2017 + INDB for Indian foods) supplies macros — don't let the LLM freehand nutrition tables for common foods.
- Use native structured outputs (Anthropic `output_config.format` json_schema / strict tools; OpenAI strict mode) instead of 'return JSON please' — but validate semantics client-side (confidence 0-1, macros ≥ 0, total ≈ Σ items) because constrained decoding guarantees shape, not truth; auto-correct totals in code, and repair-retry once with the validator error appended.
- For consistency: temperature 0-0.2 on extraction calls where supported — but note the newest Claude models (Opus 4.7+, Sonnet 5) reject temperature/top_p entirely and Anthropic has no seed param, so determinism must come from tight prompts + schema; treat ±10% retry variance as normal and never re-run vision when a user edits quantity (scale macros linearly in code).
- The single-clarifying-question slot is the highest-leverage UX+accuracy feature: target the details that swing calories >25% (ghee tadka vs plain dal, sweet vs salted lassi, fried vs plain, restaurant vs home) — but ALWAYS return a best estimate immediately; never block logging on the answer.
- Indian food needs explicit unit priors in the prompt: 1 katori = 150 ml, home roti 40 g (~105 kcal) vs restaurant tandoori 60-80 g, tadka oil alone adds 40-60 kcal/katori, and combo phrases ('rajma chawal', 'dal roti') must expand into separate logged items — embed the 3 few-shot examples covering Hinglish input, unquantified snacking, and Western cafe items.
- Handle non-food gracefully with a structured `is_food: false` + friendly `reason` field in the SAME schema (not an error or refusal) — and treat nutrition labels/menus as first-class input the model should read (`label_read: true`), since label data beats visual estimation.
- Calibrated confidence needs rubric anchors in the prompt (0.85+ label/simple food, 0.35-0.6 mixed dishes with hidden fats, <0.35 no scale cues) — mixed dishes with hidden oil/cream are where real-world accuracy drops to 25-35% error, so route low-confidence items to the clarifying question or an edit-first UI.
- Ban generic advice in the insights prompt structurally: require every insight to cite a specific number/food/day from the user's own data, blocklist stock phrases ('stay hydrated', 'balanced diet'), mandate observation → why-it-matters-for-their-goal → one food-level swap referencing foods they actually log, and handle the <10-meals cold-start case explicitly.
- Set expectations honestly in the product: peer-reviewed accuracy is ~26-36% MAPE (comparable to human self-reporting, not better) — design the UI around fast human correction (tap-to-edit portions) rather than claiming precision.
